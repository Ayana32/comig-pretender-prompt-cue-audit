# Pre-Judge Audit Notes

These observations and interpretation limits were recorded before the main
Judge calls. They are hypotheses to inspect later, not confirmed conclusions.

## Pre-specified observations

- The naive responses average about 447 words, whereas the human anchors
  average 40.75 words. If Judges explicitly use response length as evidence,
  identification may reflect a surface shortcut rather than domain competence.
- The style-controlled responses remove bullets and headings, but comma density
  is higher than in the naive condition for six of eight questions and mean
  sentence length is also higher. If Judges treat comma-linked enumeration as
  an AI cue, the manipulation may have moved an artifact into prose rather than
  removed it.
- First-person expression is more common in the human anchors than in either
  Pretender condition. The controlled condition therefore does not move every
  measured style dimension closer to the anchors.

## Position interpretation limit

A flipped original-swapped pair is consistent with position sensitivity, but
does not isolate position bias from stochastic run-to-run variability because
each order is evaluated once. Optional repetitions of flipped cases, if later
performed, are exploratory and cannot replace the primary observations.

## Execution-day boundary

Only completeness, schema validity, selected response identity,
original-swapped consistency, and confidence differences are inspected during
the primary run. Raw justifications are retained but are not read or coded
until identifiers are masked for the later manual-coding stage.
